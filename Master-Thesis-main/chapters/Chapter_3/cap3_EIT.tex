\section{Numerical Simulation Results of EIT Cooling}
\label{sec:eit_cooling_results}
%*###############################################################

The numerical model described in Section~\ref{sec:numerical_eit} was evaluated to characterize\CCgen{capitolo 3}{le quattro sezioni non si parlano fra loro}{Contato: in tutto il capitolo ci sono \textbf{due soli} rimandi interni fra una sezione e un'altra. Le quattro parti si leggono come quattro relazioni indipendenti, mentre fisicamente sono incatenate: la temperatura della melassa entra nel modello di cattura dell'ODT, il numero di atomi caricati e' la condizione iniziale del trasporto, la frequenza assiale che fissa il parametro di Lamb--Dicke di questa simulazione discende dalla profondita' del reticolo, e la stabilita' del ramo in fibra e' quella che determinera' se il trasporto conserva quel numero di atomi. Bastano tre o quattro frasi di raccordo, una per giunzione, per trasformare quattro sezioni in un capitolo.} the Electromagnetically Induced Transparency (EIT) cooling dynamics of $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ atoms. To distinguish the fundamental cooling mechanism from the effects of the complex atomic structure, the simulations were performed in two stages. First, an idealized three-level $\Lambda$ system was simulated to validate the analytical predictions. Subsequently, a complete 24-level model incorporating the full hyperfine and Zeeman structure of the $D_2$ line was implemented to assess realistic experimental conditions, including off-resonant excitation and population leakage.

The steady-state spectroscopic response of the ideal three-level system was obtained by scanning the probe-laser detuning $\Delta_p$ while keeping the coupling field fixed. The resulting total excited-state population exhibits the characteristic Fano profile associated with EIT. Starting from an initial thermal state, the system undergoes rapid ground-state cooling, decaying exponentially and approaching the theoretical minimum limit in less than 0.1 ms. This confirms the efficiency of the targeted sideband asymmetry in the absence of competing decay channels.

To demonstrate the necessity of repopulating the active cooling cycle via the repump field, the dynamics were first simulated without an active repump field, and then compared with the full optimized sequence. The impact of optical pumping is evident in the steady-state spectroscopy (Figure~\ref{fig:fano_24_comparison}). Without a repump field, the excited-state population drops to near zero across the entire frequency scan. Following a few excitation cycles, the atoms decay into uncoupled Zeeman sublevels, depleting the active EIT levels.

By introducing a $\pi$-polarized repump field, the atoms are recycled back into the EIT cycle. The Fano profile and the dark resonance are recovered, allowing the alignment of the red sideband with the absorption peak. However, compared to the three-level model, the resonance is broader and the transparency minimum is less deep, owing to off-resonant coupling to adjacent excited manifolds and the continuous scattering induced by the repump field.

This optical pumping mechanism is directly observable in the time evolution of the internal atomic populations (Figure~\ref{fig:populations_comparison}). Without the repumper, the active ground state populations decay rapidly, accumulating entirely in the uncoupled states within a few milliseconds. Conversely, activating the repump field sustains the population within the active states throughout the evolution, continuously recovering atoms that leak out of the ideal cooling cycle.

While these results validate the EIT cooling scheme within the complete atomic level structure, the simulation must eventually be adapted to reflect the exact conditions of the fiber implementation. Atoms confined near the core surface of the hollow-core fiber will experience spatially varying tensor light shifts, local magnetic field gradients, and specific polarization constraints due to the fiber's geometry. Future modeling efforts will need to incorporate these physical perturbations into the Hamiltonian to fully optimize the laser parameters and ensure robust cooling in the final experimental environment.
