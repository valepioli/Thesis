Starting from an initial value of approximately $20~\mu\mathrm{K}$, the optimization procedure minimized the optical molasses temperature. Under the final conditions, a minimum average temperature of $T=(8.42\pm0.17)~\mu\mathrm{K}$ was achieved.

The molasses temperature was determined from the ballistic expansion of the atomic cloud. Figure~\ref{fig:exp_dem} presents fluorescence images of the freely falling atomic cloud recorded at different TOFs under the optimized conditions. The cloud widths extracted from Gaussian fits were then fitted according to equation~\eqref{eq:ballistic_expansion}, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:temp_fit}.

The temperatures extracted along the two Cartesian directions are $T_x=(7.92\pm0.20)~\mu\mathrm{K}$ and $T_y=(8.91\pm0.28)~\mu\mathrm{K}$. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:temp_fit}, the measurements at long TOF deviate from the linear trend and lie above the fitted values. The same behavior was observed systematically throughout the measurement series. A plausible explanation is that, after sufficiently long expansion times, the cloud radius is no longer negligible compared with the probe beam radius, so that the assumption of uniform illumination is no longer valid. Atoms in the outer region of the cloud are therefore exposed to a weaker probe intensity, leading the Gaussian fit to underestimate the actual spatial variance. This effect should become less relevant as the atomic temperature decreases.

\subsection{Detuning frequency optimization}
%*###############################################################

Starting from an initial molasses temperature of approximately $20~\mu\mathrm{K}$, the cooling laser detuning was optimized in order to reach the final temperature reported above. The detuning was scanned from $-24.4$~MHz ($4.0~\Gamma/2\pi$) to $-184$~MHz ($30.4~\Gamma/2\pi$) in steps of $-11.4$~MHz.

Figure~\ref{fig:detuning_molasses_temperature} shows the measured average temperature as a function of the absolute detuning, expressed in units of $\Gamma/2\pi$. For relatively small detunings, the measurements exhibit the $1/\vert{}\delta\vert{}$ scaling expected in the sub-Doppler cooling regime. At larger detunings, two additional effects become important: the optical scattering rate decreases rapidly as $\Gamma_p \propto 1/\delta^2$, while the cooling frequency approaches the $F=2 \rightarrow F'=2$ transition, which lies $267$~MHz below the $F=2 \rightarrow F'=3$ cooling transition (see Figure~\ref{fig:lines}). The combined effect causes the temperature curve to level off around $\vert{}\delta\vert{}\approx15-20$ and to increase again at still larger detunings.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{Cap_3_figures/detuning_opt_fit.png}
\caption{Optical molasses temperature as a function of the cooling laser detuning. The horizontal axis shows the detuning $\vert{}\delta\vert{}$, normalized to the natural linewidth $\Gamma/2\pi$. The most effective sub-Doppler cooling is observed in the interval $\vert{}\delta\vert{}\approx15-20$, where a minimum temperature of $14.9 \pm 0.4~\mu\mathrm{K}$ is reached. Up to approximately $\vert{}\delta\vert{}=18$, the temperature follows the $1/\vert{}\delta\vert{}$ scaling predicted for ideal Sisyphus cooling.}
\label{fig:detuning_molasses_temperature}
\end{figure}

The minimum average temperature was measured at a normalized detuning of $\vert{}\delta\vert{}=17.2$, corresponding to an absolute frequency detuning of $\vert{}\delta\vert{}=104.2$~MHz. The detuning optimization therefore reduced the molasses temperature from its initial value of approximately $20~\mu\mathrm{K}$ to the final value of $T=(14.9\pm0.4)~\mu\mathrm{K}$.

To quantitatively compare the sub-Doppler cooling performance with existing literature, a fit was performed up to $\vert{}\delta\vert{} = 18$. The model fitted to the data is $T = a \frac{\Gamma}{\vert{}\delta\vert{}} + T_0$.

Following the framework by Gerz et al.~\cite{Gerz:93}, the temperature of a 3D optical molasses scales linearly with the AC Stark shift as $\frac{k_B T}{\hbar \Gamma} = c_{\perp} \frac{\Omega^2}{\vert{}\delta\vert{} \Gamma} + \text{const}$. The fit parameter $a$ is thus related to the dimensionless constant $c_{\perp}$ by $c_{\perp} = \frac{a \cdot k_B}{\hbar \Gamma} (\frac{\Gamma^2}{\Omega^2})$.

\subsection{AOM amplitude optimization}
%*###############################################################
The second stage of the optimization focused on the effect of the cooling beam power. This was investigated by varying both the AOM drive amplitude and the duration of the amplitude ramp applied at the beginning of the molasses phase. The AOM amplitude, given in arbitrary units (a.u.), determines the RF amplitude used to drive the modulator. Three settings were examined: 385, 475, and 550~a.u., \CCerr{corresponding respectively to cooling laser powers of 5.8~mW, 6.6~mW, and 6.3~mW}{Da controllare, perche' cosi' com'e' sembra un refuso. La potenza non e' monotona nell'ampiezza AOM (385 $\to$ 5.8~mW, 475 $\to$ 6.6~mW, 550 $\to$ 6.3~mW): il massimo cade nel mezzo. E' fisicamente possibile, perche' l'efficienza di diffrazione dell'AOM satura e poi cala, ed e' proprio quello che la sezione sull'ODT dice (``near the saturation region of the AOM diffraction-efficiency curve''); ma qui non e' detto, e il lettore legge una serie non monotona senza spiegazione. Basta una riga. Nota anche che il MOT gira a 550 a.u., cioe' a potenza MINORE di 475 a.u.} measured after the collimator of the MOT $z$-beam. The MOT itself is operated at an AOM amplitude of 550~a.u.

Ramp durations of 5~ms and 20~ms were also compared for the transition between the MOT and molasses amplitude settings. The measurements were repeated at several values of the cooling laser detuning.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{Cap_3_figures/aom_amplitude_comparison.png}
    \caption{Optical molasses temperature as a function of cooling laser detuning for different AOM amplitudes and ramp durations. No clear optimum in the AOM amplitude is visible, since the measured variations remain within the experimental uncertainties. In contrast, the temperatures obtained with a 20~ms ramp (blue lines) are systematically lower than those measured with a 5~ms ramp (red lines).}
    \label{fig:aom_amp_and_ramp_MOL_opt}
\end{figure}

The results are summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:aom_amp_and_ramp_MOL_opt}. Although modest differences in the measured temperature are visible among the tested AOM amplitudes, they remain compatible within the experimental uncertainties. Consequently, no statistically meaningful dependence of the molasses temperature on the AOM amplitude could be established, and no unique optimal value was identified over the range considered.

By contrast, Figure~\ref{fig:aom_amp_and_ramp_MOL_opt} shows that the 20~ms ramp consistently produces lower temperatures than the 5~ms ramp, with an average reduction of approximately $0.5~\mu\mathrm{K}$. Since this improvement was modest but adequate for the experimental objectives, \CCn{additional ramp durations were not explored}{Il valore scelto sta sul bordo dell'intervallo esplorato: sono stati confrontati solo 5 e 20 ms, e 20 ms e' il piu' lungo dei due e il migliore dei due. Un valore migliore al bordo non e' un ottimo, e' un limite della scansione, e converrebbe dirlo. Vale anche per l'ampiezza AOM: 385 a.u. e' la piu' bassa delle tre provate ed e' quella scelta. E c'e' una tensione con la frase precedente: se la dipendenza dall'ampiezza non e' statisticamente significativa, scegliere ``quella che ha dato la temperatura piu' bassa'' significa selezionare sul rumore.}. A ramp time of 20~ms was therefore adopted for the following measurements.

During the subsequent characterization of the ODT loading, however, this configuration was found to introduce additional fluctuations in the system. The compensation coil currents were therefore optimized using an AOM amplitude of 385~a.u., which yielded the lowest measured temperature at $|\delta|=17.2~\Gamma/2\pi$, together with a ramp duration of 20~ms. These settings were later modified during the optimization of the ODT loading. Nevertheless, the measurements discussed here indicate that changes in these parameters affect the molasses temperature only at the level of a few tenths of a microkelvin and therefore have no substantial impact on the minimum temperature ultimately achieved.

\subsection{Compensation field optimization}
%*###############################################################
The final stage of the molasses optimization consisted of adjusting the magnetic compensation field. The currents supplied to the compensation coils were switched to their molasses values 5~ms after the MOT magnetic field was turned off.

\CCn{The three current components were optimized sequentially}{Una sola passata, senza iterazione, e i numeri dicono che converrebbe rifarla. Lo $z$ e' stato ottimizzato mentre $x$ era ancora al valore preliminare, e $x$ e' poi cambiato di 95~mA (da $+80$ a $-15$); $z$ stesso e' cambiato di 170~mA. Poiche' quello che conta e' il modulo del campo residuo, i tre assi sono accoppiati e l'ottimo di $z$ trovato con $x$ sbagliato di 95~mA non e' necessariamente l'ottimo finale. Una seconda passata costa poco e chiude la questione.}: the $z$-axis current was adjusted first, while the $x$- and $y$-axis currents were held at the preliminary values given in equation~\eqref{eq:shim_preliminary_values}. The $x$-axis current was then optimized using the newly determined value of the $z$ component. Finally, the $y$-axis current was adjusted while keeping both the $x$- and $z$-axis currents at their optimized settings. The experimental data were fitted with quadratic functions to determine the optimal currents and the associated minimum temperatures. \CCref{The results for the $z$-, $x$-, and $y$-axis coils are presented in Figure~\ref{fig:shim_opt}(a), (b), and (c), respectively.}{Le sottofigure hanno le loro etichette (\texttt{fig:shimz\_opt}, \texttt{fig:shimx\_opt}, \texttt{fig:shimy\_opt}) ma non vengono mai usate: qui le lettere (a), (b), (c) sono scritte a mano. Funziona, ma se l'ordine delle sottofigure cambia le lettere nel testo non seguono. Con \texttt{subcaption} basta \texttt{\textbackslash ref\{fig:shimz\_opt\}} e la lettera viene generata da sola. Stessa situazione altrove: etichette di sottofigura mai usate in appendice (i tre segnali di shim), in \texttt{cap2\_BLACK\_setup} e in \texttt{cap3\_ODT\_LOAD}.}

The optimized current settings subsequently used during ODT loading were:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{cases}
        I_{x} &= -15~\mathrm{mA}, \\
        I_{y} &= -185~\mathrm{mA}, \\
        I_{z} &= -230~\mathrm{mA}.
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:mol_shim_optimal_values}
\end{equation}

Using the calibration reported in Section~\ref{subsec:setup}, these currents correspond to the following magnetic field components:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{cases}
        B_{x} &= -21~\mathrm{mG}, \\
        B_{y} &= -241~\mathrm{mG}, \\
        B_{z} &= -345~\mathrm{mG}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

With these parameters, the molasses temperature was reduced to $T=(8.4\pm0.2)~\mu\mathrm{K}$. The largest correction relative to the preliminary settings occurred along the $z$ axis. A smaller, though still substantial, adjustment was required for the $x$-axis current, whereas the optimized $y$-axis current remained close to its initial value.

The operating values were chosen to be integers close to the fitted minima. Since in all three cases these settings lie near the vertex of the fitted parabola, the slight deviation from the exact optimal values is not expected to introduce a significant systematic contribution to the temperature measurements.
