\section{Numerical Model of EIT Cooling}
\label{sec:numerical_eit}

Once the atoms enter the fiber, they must be efficiently cooled to control their motion\CCgen{sezione sul modello numerico}{e' un capitolo di metodi dentro un capitolo di apparato}{Questa sezione ha \textbf{42 equazioni}, piu' di qualsiasi altra della tesi, e 3 citazioni; il resto del capitolo 2 descrive banchi ottici e procedure di misura. Sono due generi diversi di ``metodo'' messi sotto lo stesso titolo, e la sezione ne esce penalizzata: il lettore ci arriva con l'aspettativa sbagliata. Due possibilita', entrambe legittime: farne un capitolo a se (``Modello numerico''), oppure lasciarla qui ma aprirla con un capoverso che dichiari il cambio di registro, che cosa il modello prende come dato dall'esperimento e che cosa restituisce al capitolo 3. La seconda costa una riga. Aggiungo che e' anche la sezione che contiene la riserva piu' importante di tutta la parte numerica, quella sul rinculo dell'emissione spontanea trascurato, e in un capitolo separato quella riserva sarebbe molto piu' visibile.} (Sec.~\ref{sec:EIT_cooling}). Simulating this process is a necessary step before modeling the atoms' behavior in the full experimental system. The EIT cooling dynamics are simulated using QuTiP~\cite{Johansson2012}, solving the open-system evolution of an atom coupled to a quantized motional mode. Two models of increasing complexity are implemented. The first is an ideal three-level $\Lambda$ system, used to validate the EIT cooling mechanism and the numerical implementation against the analytical predictions of Sec.~\ref{sec:EIT_cooling}. The second includes the complete hyperfine and Zeeman structure of the $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ $D_2$ line, and is used to assess off-resonant excitation, spontaneous emission, optical pumping, and repumping.

Both descriptions are formulated within the same Lindblad master-equation framework and differ only in the dimension and structure of the internal atomic Hilbert space. The motion is treated in one spatial dimension throughout; the simulated direction is set by projecting each laser's wavevector onto the corresponding trap axis.

\subsection{Internal Atomic Basis}
\label{subsec:atomic_basis}

In the reduced model, the internal Hilbert space is spanned by two ground states, $|g_1\rangle$ and $|g_2\rangle$, and one excited state, $|e\rangle$. A weak probe field with Rabi frequency $\Omega_p$ couples $|g_1\rangle$ to $|e\rangle$, while a stronger coupling field with Rabi frequency $\Omega_c$ couples $|g_2\rangle$ to $|e\rangle$. At two-photon resonance this model supports the dark state of Eq.~\eqref{eq:darkstate} and reproduces the ideal carrier suppression discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:EIT_cooling}. The three-level calculation is used to test the Hamiltonian construction, the motional coupling, the collapse operators, and the time-evolution routines before introducing the complete atomic structure.

The multilevel model includes all hyperfine and Zeeman sublevels of the $5S_{1/2}$ and $5P_{3/2}$ manifolds of $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$. The internal basis is written as $|F,m_F\rangle$ and comprises the ground manifolds
\begin{equation}
    F=1,\qquad F=2,
\end{equation}
with three and five Zeeman states respectively, and the excited manifolds
\begin{equation}
    F'=0,1,2,3,
\end{equation}
containing sixteen states in total. The dimension of the internal Hilbert space is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \dim\!\left(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{at}}\right)=3+5+16=24.
\end{equation}

All dipole-allowed couplings are constructed explicitly. For a transition between a ground state $|F_g,m_g\rangle$ and an excited state $|F_e,m_e\rangle$, driven by a spherical polarization component $q\in\{-1,0,+1\}$, the coupling strength is calculated from the hyperfine dipole matrix element~\cite{SteckRb87},
\begin{equation}
    d_{eg}^{(q)}
    =
    \langle F_e,m_e|d_q|F_g,m_g\rangle
    =
    \langle F_e\Vert d\Vert F_g\rangle
    (-1)^{F_e-m_e}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        F_e & 1 & F_g\\
        -m_e & q & m_g
    \end{pmatrix},
    \label{eq:dipole_matrix_multilevel}
\end{equation}
where the $3j$ symbol carries the electric-dipole selection rules, \CCn{and the reduced hyperfine matrix element $\langle F_e\Vert d\Vert F_g\rangle$ sets the relative strength between transitions connecting different hyperfine manifolds, through}{L'espressione che segue e' quella di Steck, ma li' e' scritta per l'elemento ridotto con lo stato FONDAMENTALE nel bra, $\langle F_g\Vert d\Vert F_e\rangle$, mentre qui il membro sinistro ha $e$ e $g$ scambiati e il membro destro e' copiato invariato. Gli elementi ridotti non sono simmetrici: scambiando bra e ket si prende un fattore $(-1)^{F_g-F_e}$. Non cambia nessun risultato, perche' e' una fase globale per ogni coppia di multipletti e nel tuo caso si riassorbe ridefinendo la fase di $|g_2\rangle$, quindi lo stato oscuro e la trasparenza restano quelli. Ma un lettore che confronta con la referenza che citi si ferma qui. Nota che l'equazione precedente, quella di Wigner-Eckart con il $3j$, usa coerentemente $\langle F_e\Vert d\Vert F_g\rangle$: e' solo questa seconda a portare la convenzione dell'altra fonte, quindi basta girare il membro sinistro o aggiungere la fase.}
\begin{equation}
    \langle F_e\Vert d\Vert F_g\rangle
    =
    \langle J_e\Vert d\Vert J_g\rangle
    (-1)^{F_e+J_g+1+I}
    \sqrt{(2F_e+1)(2J_g+1)}
    \begin{Bmatrix}
        J_g & J_e & 1\\
        F_e & F_g & I
    \end{Bmatrix},
    \label{eq:reduced_hyperfine_element}
\end{equation}
with $J_g=1/2$, $J_e=3/2$, and $I=3/2$ for the $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ $D_2$ line. Transitions that violate the electric-dipole selection rules are assigned zero coupling.

To reduce optical pumping into states outside the EIT cycle, the target $\Lambda$ system is built around states near the edge of a Zeeman manifold rather than around $m_F=0$: the condition $\Delta m_F=0,\pm1$ then limits the number of ground states reachable by spontaneous decay. In the configuration used for the multilevel and axial-parameter simulations (Fig.~\ref{fig:multilevel_EIT_scheme}), the two EIT ground states belong to different hyperfine manifolds. The $\sigma^-$-polarized probe field addresses $|g_1\rangle=|F=2,m_F=-1\rangle\rightarrow|F'=2,m_F=-2\rangle$ and is scanned across the two-photon resonance. The $\sigma^-$-polarized coupling field addresses $|g_2\rangle=|F=1,m_F=-1\rangle\rightarrow|F'=2,m_F=-2\rangle$; its Rabi frequency is set by the EIT cooling condition of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_condition_numerical}. A $\pi$-polarized repump field, resonant on $|F=2,m_F=-2\rangle\rightarrow|F'=2,m_F=-2\rangle$, \CCn{recovers population that leaks out of the $\Lambda$ system into the adjacent Zeeman sublevel of $F=2$}{Controllato canale per canale, e il sistema si chiude esattamente: e' un punto forte dello schema che meriti di rivendicare invece di lasciare implicito. Da $|F'=2,m_F=-2\rangle$ il decadimento spontaneo ha $\Delta m_F=0,\pm1$, quindi $m_F\in\{-1,-2,-3\}$, e $\Delta F=0,\pm1$ lascia solo $F=1,2$. Restano tre stati: $|F=2,m_F=-1\rangle$ che e' $|g_1\rangle$, $|F=1,m_F=-1\rangle$ che e' $|g_2\rangle$, e $|F=2,m_F=-2\rangle$. Due righe con questa enumerazione rendono evidente perche' hai scelto il bordo del multipletto invece di $m_F=0$.}; its motional factor is set to the identity, Eq.~\eqref{eq:repump_no_motion}. This configuration is used for the simulation with the axial trap parameters (Sec.~\ref{subsec:axial_simulation}).

\subsection{Laser Coupling and Lamb--Dicke Expansion}
\label{subsec:laser_coupling}

For a laser field $l$ with wavevector $\mathbf{k}_l$, optical phase $\phi_l$, and polarization components $\epsilon_{l,q}$, the position-dependent rotating-wave interaction is written as
\begin{equation}
    H_l
    =
    \frac{\hbar}{2}
    \sum_{g,e,q}
    \Omega_l\epsilon_{l,q}\,
    \mathcal C_{eg}^{(q)}
    |e\rangle\langle g|\,
    e^{i k_{l,\parallel}x+i\phi_l}
    +
    \mathrm{h.c.},
    \label{eq:general_laser_interaction}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal C_{eg}^{(q)}=d_{eg}^{(q)}/d_{\mathrm{target}}$ is the dipole matrix element of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dipole_matrix_multilevel}, normalized to the value $d_{\mathrm{target}}$ of the transition each beam is designed to drive, and
\begin{equation}
    k_{l,\parallel}
    =
    \mathbf{k}_l\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}_{\mathrm{mot}}
\end{equation}
is the projection of the laser wavevector along the simulated motional axis.

Using
\begin{equation}
    x=x_0(a+a^\dagger),
    \qquad
    x_0=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\nu}},
\end{equation}
the Lamb--Dicke parameter associated with field $l$ is
\begin{equation}
    \eta_l=k_{l,\parallel}x_0.
    \label{eq:eta_individual_beam}
\end{equation}
In the Lamb--Dicke regime, the position-dependent phase is expanded to first order,
\begin{equation}
    e^{ik_{l,\parallel}x}
    \simeq
    1+i\eta_l(a+a^\dagger).
    \label{eq:LD_expansion_numerical}
\end{equation}
This expansion retains the carrier and first motional sidebands while neglecting higher-order processes.

For a Raman process involving absorption from one field and stimulated emission into another, the two-photon momentum transfer is set by
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathbf{k}
    =
    \mathbf{k}_p-\mathbf{k}_c,
\end{equation}
consistent with Sec.~\ref{sec:EIT_cooling}. The corresponding effective Lamb--Dicke parameter along the simulated direction is
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\mathrm{eff}}
    =
    (\mathbf{k}_p-\mathbf{k}_c)\cdot
    \hat{\mathbf e}_{\mathrm{mot}}\,x_0.
    \label{eq:eta_effective_numerical}
\end{equation}
In the three-level benchmark, the coupling beam propagates orthogonally to the motional axis, so $\eta_c=0$, while the probe field carries the motional coupling. In the multilevel model the framework allows an independent value of $\eta_l$ for each beam, according to the experimental geometry.

\subsection{Hamiltonian in the Rotating Frame}
\label{subsec:rotating_hamiltonian}

After transformation to a rotating frame and application of the rotating-wave approximation, the Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
    H
    =
    H_{\mathrm{at}}
    +
    H_{\mathrm{mot}}
    +
    H_{\mathrm{Z}}
    +
    \sum_l H_l.
    \label{eq:total_hamiltonian_numerical}
\end{equation}
Here $H_{\mathrm{at}}$ contains the hyperfine energies and laser detunings in the selected rotating frame, and
\begin{equation}
    H_{\mathrm{Z}}
    =
    \sum_i
    g_{F_i}\mu_B B m_{F_i}
    |i\rangle\langle i|
    \otimes\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mot}}
    \label{eq:zeeman_hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
describes the linear Zeeman shift produced by the quantization field $B$, assumed weak enough that $F$ and $m_F$ remain good labels.

\subsection{Spontaneous Emission and Master Equation}
\label{subsec:master_equation_numerical}

The density operator evolves according to
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\rho}{dt}
    =
    -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H,\rho]
    +
    \sum_k
    \left(
        L_k\rho L_k^\dagger
        -
        \frac{1}{2}
        L_k^\dagger L_k\rho
        -
        \frac{1}{2}
        \rho L_k^\dagger L_k
    \right).
    \label{eq:lindblad_numerical}
\end{equation}
For the three-level system, symmetric branching into the two ground states is assumed:
\begin{equation}
    L_1
    =
    \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,
    |g_1\rangle\langle e|
    \otimes\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mot}},
    \qquad
    L_2
    =
    \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\,
    |g_2\rangle\langle e|
    \otimes\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mot}}.
    \label{eq:three_level_collapse}
\end{equation}

For the multilevel system, the collapse operator for a decay channel $e\rightarrow g$ is
\begin{equation}
    L_{e\rightarrow g}
    =
    \sqrt{\gamma_e b_{eg}}\,
    |g\rangle\langle e|
    \otimes\mathbb I_{\mathrm{mot}},
    \label{eq:multilevel_collapse}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_e$ is the total decay rate of the excited state and $b_{eg}$ the normalized branching fraction,
\begin{equation}
    b_{eg}
    =
    \frac{\displaystyle
        \sum_q\left|d_{eg}^{(q)}\right|^2}
    {\displaystyle
        \sum_{g',q}\left|d_{eg'}^{(q)}\right|^2},
    \qquad
    \sum_g b_{eg}=1,
    \label{eq:branching_fraction}
\end{equation}
so that, in general,
\begin{equation}
    \sum_g
    L_{e\rightarrow g}^\dagger
    L_{e\rightarrow g}
    =
    \gamma_e|e\rangle\langle e|.
\end{equation}
With the full hyperfine-weighted dipole matrix elements of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dipole_matrix_multilevel}, $b_{eg}$ is normalized per excited sublevel by construction, Eq.~\eqref{eq:branching_fraction}, so the identity above holds exactly, with $\gamma_e=\gamma$ for every excited hyperfine sublevel. The total decay rate is therefore independent of $F'$, consistent with the physical picture of a single natural linewidth for the $D_2$ line.

The model accounts for motional changes driven by laser absorption and stimulated coupling, \CCerr{but not for recoil heating during spontaneous emission}{Questa e' la riserva piu' importante di tutta la parte numerica, ed e' anche l'unica volta in cui compare. Il modello omette il riscaldamento da rinculo dell'emissione spontanea, e scrivi tu stessa che ``it can still affect the final occupation and should be included for a quantitative comparison with the theoretical limit''. Ma l'occupazione finale e' esattamente cio' che il capitolo 3 (``bringing the atoms toward the motional ground state'', $\langle n\rangle\ll1$), le conclusioni e l'abstract (``can successfully bring the atoms near the motional ground state'') affermano. Il valore stazionario di $\langle n\rangle$ nasce dall'equilibrio fra raffreddamento e riscaldamento: se manca un canale di riscaldamento, il $\langle n\rangle$ simulato e' per costruzione un limite inferiore. La riserva va portata nel capitolo 3 e almeno accennata nelle conclusioni; ed e' probabilmente anche il motivo per cui un valore numerico di $\langle n\rangle$ non viene mai dato.}. Within the Lamb--Dicke regime this correction enters at order $\eta^2$; it can still affect the final occupation and should be included for a quantitative comparison with the theoretical limit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:n_min} or with experimental temperatures.

\subsection{Steady-State Spectroscopy}
\label{subsec:steady_state_spectroscopy}

The EIT spectrum is calculated by scanning the probe detuning while holding the remaining laser parameters fixed. At each detuning, the stationary density operator is obtained from
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_{\mathrm{tot}}(\rho_{\mathrm{ss}})=0,
    \qquad
    \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{ss}})=1,
    \label{eq:steady_state_condition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is the full Liouvillian superoperator.

The main spectral observable is the total excited-state population,
\begin{equation}
    P_e(\Delta_p)
    =
    \mathrm{Tr}
    \left[
        \rho_{\mathrm{ss}}(\Delta_p)
        \sum_{e}
        |e\rangle\langle e|
    \right].
    \label{eq:excited_population_spectrum}
\end{equation}
In the weak-probe regime this is proportional to the total photon-scattering rate and is used as an absorption proxy.

For the multilevel calculation, additional projectors distinguish excitation from population leakage:
\begin{align}
    P_{F'=2}
    &=
    \sum_{e\in F'=2}
    |e\rangle\langle e|,
    \\
    P_{F'=3}
    &=
    \sum_{e\in F'=3}
    |e\rangle\langle e|,
    \\
    P_{\mathrm{out}}
    &=
    \sum_{g\notin\mathcal G_{\Lambda}}
    |g\rangle\langle g|,
    \label{eq:diagnostic_projectors}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal G_{\Lambda}$ is the pair of ground states defining the target $\Lambda$ system. These observables identify the dark resonance, quantify off-resonant excitation, and track population accumulated in ground states not directly addressed by the EIT beams.

\subsection{Time-Dependent Cooling Dynamics}
\label{subsec:time_dynamics_numerical}

Cooling is quantified by propagating an initial atom--motion state and evaluating the phonon number expectation value,
\begin{equation}
    \langle n(t) \rangle
    =
    \mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho(t) a^\dagger a\right].
    \label{eq:n_expectation_numerical}
\end{equation}
The initial internal state is chosen within the active ground-state manifold. The motional state is specified either as a Fock state or as a thermal mixture,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{th}}
    =
    \sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{vib}}-1}
    p_n|n\rangle\langle n|,
    \qquad
    p_n
    =
    \frac{\bar n_0^n}{(1+\bar n_0)^{n+1}},
    \label{eq:thermal_initial_state}
\end{equation}
with the probabilities renormalized after truncation when necessary.

The three-level model is propagated with QuTiP's deterministic master-equation solver \texttt{mesolve}, which evolves the density operator directly and gives noise-free ensemble expectation values. For the full multilevel model, the Monte Carlo wavefunction solver \texttt{mcsolve} is used instead~\cite{Johansson2012}: it propagates stochastic pure-state trajectories interrupted by quantum jumps, estimating an observable as
\begin{equation}
    \langle O(t)\rangle
    \simeq
    \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{traj}}}
    \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{traj}}}
    \langle\psi_j(t)|O|\psi_j(t)\rangle.
    \label{eq:mc_average}
\end{equation}
The statistical uncertainty decreases approximately as $N_{\mathrm{traj}}^{-1/2}$.

For internal dimension $N_{\mathrm{at}}$ and motional cutoff $N_{\mathrm{vib}}$, the state-vector dimension is
\begin{equation}
    N=N_{\mathrm{at}}N_{\mathrm{vib}}.
\end{equation}
A density matrix has $N^2$ complex entries, a Monte Carlo trajectory a state vector with $N$ entries; the trajectory method substantially reduces memory use for the 24-level calculation.

\subsection{Units and Conversion to Physical Time}
\label{subsec:numerical_units}

The numerical calculations use the natural angular linewidth as the unit of frequency,
\begin{equation}
    \gamma=1,
\end{equation}
with $\hbar=1$. All detunings, Rabi frequencies, hyperfine splittings, Zeeman shifts, and trap frequencies are divided by $\gamma$, and time is expressed in units of $\gamma^{-1}$.

For the $^{87}\mathrm{Rb}$ $D_2$ transition, the linewidth is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\gamma}{2\pi}=6.067~\mathrm{MHz},
\end{equation}
so one simulation time unit corresponds to
\begin{equation}
    \gamma^{-1}
    =
    \frac{1}{2\pi\times6.067~\mathrm{MHz}}
    \simeq
    26.2~\mathrm{ns}.
    \label{eq:time_conversion}
\end{equation}
A dimensionless interval $t_{\mathrm{sim}}$ converts to physical time through
\begin{equation}
    t_{\mathrm{phys}}
    =
    \frac{t_{\mathrm{sim}}}{\gamma}.
    \label{eq:physical_time_conversion}
\end{equation}
The same angular-frequency convention is used for $\nu$, $\Omega_l$, $\Delta_l$, and the Zeeman shifts, avoiding factors of $2\pi$ inside the Hamiltonian.

\subsection{Validation of the Three-Level Model}
\label{subsec:three_level_validation}

The reduced model is validated in two stages. First, the motional degree of freedom is omitted and the probe detuning is scanned to verify the EIT transparency minimum at two-photon resonance and the narrow dressed-state resonance displaced by the coupling-induced AC Stark shift of Eq.~\eqref{eq:acstarkshift}.

Second, the motional basis is restored and the coupling intensity is set by the EIT cooling condition. For the sign convention and blue-detuned configuration of Sec.~\ref{sec:EIT_cooling}, the exact relation is Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_cooling_condition},
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_c^2=4\nu(\nu+\Delta_c),
    \label{eq:exact_condition_numerical}
\end{equation}
which reduces to
\begin{equation}
    \Omega_c^2\simeq4\Delta_c\nu
    \label{eq:approx_condition_numerical}
\end{equation}
when $\nu\ll\Delta_c$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact_condition_numerical} sets $\Omega_c$ in both the three-level and multilevel codes. Under this condition, the narrow absorption resonance aligns with the red sideband while carrier excitation is suppressed by the dark resonance.

\subsection{Optimization of the Multilevel Configuration}
\label{subsec:multilevel_optimization}

The multilevel laser parameters are optimized on steady-state spectra before running the more expensive time-dependent simulations. For each parameter set, the probe detuning is scanned and the following are recorded: the total excited-state population, the populations of the target and off-resonant excited manifolds, and the population outside the active $\Lambda$ subsystem.

The scans proceed sequentially:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The repump Rabi frequency is varied to find the minimum intensity that prevents long-lived population accumulation outside the EIT cycle. Excessive repump power is avoided, since off-resonant coupling can shift and broaden the EIT spectrum.

    \item The probe Rabi frequency is varied within the weak-probe regime. Increasing $\Omega_p$ increases the scattering signal and may increase the cooling rate, but it also saturates and power-broadens the dark resonance, increasing carrier scattering.

    \item The coupling detuning and Rabi frequency are adjusted to align the narrow absorption maximum with the red sideband.
\end{enumerate}

The optimization criterion is not the height of the absorption peak alone: parameter sets are chosen for low carrier excitation, enhanced scattering at the cooling sideband, reduced scattering at the heating sideband, and limited population leakage. The selected parameters are then tested through a time-dependent \texttt{mcsolve} calculation of $\langle n(t)\rangle$.
